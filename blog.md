## Blog 1: Why Learning Data Science in Social and Interdisciplinary Contexts Matters
Learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts matters because data is not just numbers. It often represents real people, their behaviors, experiences, and decisions. When we model social topics, we can use data to better understand patterns in areas such as sports, education, business, economics, and many other parts of society. For example, data science can help a sports organization understand player performance or help a school identify trends in student success. Modeling these topics can help us make better decisions and recognize patterns that might not be obvious without data.

At the same time, using data about people comes with a lot of responsibility. One responsibility is making sure the data is being used ethically and respectfully. People may not always realize how their information is being collected or how it could be used. Data scientists also need to think about privacy and security, especially when working with personal information. Another responsibility is recognizing bias. If the data we use is biased or incomplete, the model we create can produce unfair or misleading results. It is important to understand that a model is not automatically correct just because it is based on data.

I think learning data science from an interdisciplinary perspective is valuable because problems in the real world rarely fit into one subject. Data science can connect technology, business, sports, psychology, economics, and many other fields. As someone interested in sports and sports analytics, I can see how these skills could be useful for understanding player performance, team decisions, fan behavior, and other areas of the sports industry.

Through this course, I am hoping to better understand how to take real world data and turn it into information that is easy to understand and can actually be useful. I also want to become better at interpreting models and understanding what the results really mean instead of just looking at numbers. Mostly, I want to learn how to use data responsibly and understand its limitations. These skills will be important not only for this class, but also for my future career in sports analytics.
